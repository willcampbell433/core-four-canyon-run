Seafloor Reference

Ridges and lumps

The two Barnegat ridges and Seaside Lump are shallow structure close enough for a one-day inshore tuna hunt. Public waypoint references are on the chart; the boat’s chartplotter and John’s judgment remain the source of truth.

Barnegat Ridge reference Seaside Lump reference NOAA live bluefin status
Stop 1 Barnegat Ridge South Shallower southern ridge with a sharp drop nearby. First place to find bait and marks.
Stop 2 Barnegat Ridge North Broader tabletop roughly a mile north. Easy slide if the first pass is quiet.
Stop 3 Seaside Lumps Nearshore bluefin ground off Seaside Park. Current reports say the bite has been real but mobile.
The pattern

Structure holds the food

Ridges and lumps redirect current and stack bait. The public numbers get the boat into the neighborhood; sonar, water color, temperature, and visible life pick the exact pass.

  • Ridge South: work the notch and nearby drop.
  • Ridge North: cover the tabletop and either gully.
  • Seaside Lumps: stay mobile and follow the bait.

        Know Your Target

        What might eat the jig

        Bluefin tuna

        The freight train

        • ID: Dark steel-blue back, short pectoral fins, built like a torpedo.
        • Fight: Long deep runs. Keep pressure and let the harness work.
        • Eats: Trolled bars and ballyhoo, jigs on marks, live bluefish or squid.
        • Aug 7 status: 27 to under 73 inches open, max 2 per vessel/trip; only 1 may be 47 to under 73. Recheck NOAA before lines in.
        • Report: Landings and dead discards within 24 hours after returning.
        Yellowfin tuna

        The outside shot

        • ID: Bright yellow sickle fins and a gold stripe down the side.
        • Fight: Blistering first run, then stubborn circles under the boat.
        • Eats: Spreader bars, ballyhoo, poppers when they blitz.
        • Plan: Less likely on this nearshore route, but never ignore a warm-water surprise.
        Bigeye tuna

        The long shot

        • ID: Oversized eye, deeper body, longer pectorals than bluefin.
        • Fight: Pound for pound the meanest tuna in the canyon. Expect a war.
        • Eats: Low-light trolled spreads and deep marks.
        • Plan: Canyon fish, not the expected customer on this inshore board.
        Mahi mahi

        The acrobat

        • ID: Electric green and gold, blunt forehead on the bulls.
        • Fight: Jumps, flips, and never quits. Chaos on light tackle.
        • Eats: Anything near floating structure. Check every lobster pot ball and weedline.
        • Table: Fish tacos. Non-negotiable.
